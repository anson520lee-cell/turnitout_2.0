Social media has become an integral part of modern life, and its influence on young people is a topic of ongoing debate. On the one hand, platforms such as Instagram and TikTok allow teenagers to stay connected with friends and express their creativity. On the other hand, excessive use has been linked to anxiety, sleep problems, and lower self-esteem.

Moreover, social media exposes young users to unrealistic standards of beauty and success. Constant comparison with curated online personas can leave teenagers feeling inadequate. Furthermore, cyberbullying has become a serious concern, as harmful comments can spread quickly and be difficult to remove.

However, it is important to note that social media is not inherently harmful. When used mindfully, it can provide access to educational content, support communities, and opportunities for activism. Therefore, the key lies in balance and guidance from parents and schools.

In conclusion, social media plays a significant role in shaping the lives of young people. By promoting digital literacy and healthy habits, society can help ensure that its benefits outweigh its risks.

Climate change is one of the most pressing challenges facing humanity today. Rising global temperatures have led to melting ice caps, more frequent extreme weather events, and threats to biodiversity. It is essential to understand the causes and consequences of this phenomenon in order to develop effective solutions.

The primary driver of climate change is the burning of fossil fuels, which releases greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. Additionally, deforestation reduces the planet's capacity to absorb carbon dioxide. As a result, the Earth's average temperature has increased significantly since the industrial revolution.

To address this issue, governments, businesses, and individuals must work together. Renewable energy sources such as solar and wind power offer a sustainable alternative to fossil fuels. Furthermore, individuals can contribute by reducing waste, conserving energy, and supporting environmentally responsible policies.

In summary, tackling climate change requires collective action and long-term commitment. Only through cooperation can we protect the planet for future generations.

Public transport is one of the most effective ways to reduce traffic congestion and air pollution in large cities. A single bus or train can carry the same number of passengers as dozens of private cars, using far less road space and fuel. Cities with reliable transit systems also tend to have lower rates of car ownership.

Investing in public transport brings economic benefits as well. Commuters spend less on fuel and parking, and businesses gain access to a wider pool of workers. Areas around stations often see rising property values and new retail activity.

Yet many cities struggle to fund and maintain their networks. Aging infrastructure, delays, and safety concerns can discourage people from using buses and trains. Addressing these problems requires long-term planning and steady public investment. When transit is frequent, clean, and affordable, more residents choose it over driving, and the whole city benefits.

Hong Kong's housing crisis has become one of the most visible social issues in the city. Home prices are among the highest in the world relative to income, and many young people cannot afford to buy or even rent a decent flat. Subdivided units, often only a few square metres in size, house tens of thousands of families.

Several factors contribute to the problem. Land supply is limited, and the government has historically relied on land sales for revenue. Strong demand from investors has pushed prices higher, while construction of public housing has struggled to keep pace with waiting lists.

Solving the crisis will require a combination of measures. Releasing more land for development, speeding up public housing projects, and taxing speculative purchases could all ease the pressure. Equally important is the need for transparent planning so that residents can trust that reforms will last.

Sleep is essential for physical and mental health, yet many students do not get enough of it. Teenagers need around eight to ten hours each night, but surveys show that most sleep far less. Late-night studying, screen time, and early school start times all play a part.

The effects of sleep deprivation are serious. Tired students find it harder to concentrate, remember information, and regulate their emotions. Over time, poor sleep is linked to weaker immune function and a higher risk of depression.

Simple changes can make a difference. Keeping a regular bedtime, avoiding screens an hour before sleep, and limiting caffeine in the afternoon all support better rest. Schools can help by starting classes later and by teaching students why sleep matters. Treating rest as part of learning, rather than a reward for finishing work, is a healthier approach.

Why do some cities thrive while others decline? Economists point to a mix of geography, institutions, and human capital. Ports and rivers have long given certain places an edge, but natural advantages alone are not enough. Cities that attract educated workers and encourage new businesses tend to grow faster than those that do not.

Governance matters just as much. Clear property rights, efficient services, and honest administration make it easier for firms to invest. When officials are corrupt or unpredictable, capital moves elsewhere. Detroit and Singapore show how different choices can lead to very different outcomes.

None of this means that decline is inevitable. Cities can reinvent themselves by investing in education, supporting small firms, and improving quality of life. The path is rarely quick, but the evidence suggests that deliberate, sustained effort pays off.

The ethics of gene editing have moved from science fiction to urgent public debate. Tools such as CRISPR allow scientists to alter DNA with unprecedented precision, offering hope for curing inherited diseases. At the same time, the same technology could be used to select traits like height or intelligence, raising fears of a new form of inequality.

Supporters argue that preventing suffering is a moral duty. If a harmful mutation can be removed before birth, they say, society should not stand in the way. Critics respond that the line between therapy and enhancement is hard to draw, and that mistakes could be passed on to future generations.

A cautious path seems wise. Strict oversight, international agreement, and open public discussion can allow beneficial uses while guarding against abuse. The science will keep advancing, so the real question is how wisely we choose to use it.

## The Impact of Remote Work on Productivity

Remote work has reshaped the modern workplace, and its effect on productivity is nuanced.

**Key benefits:**
* Employees save commuting time, which can be used for focused work or rest.
* Flexible schedules let people work during their most productive hours.
* Companies can hire from a global talent pool.

**Key challenges:**
* Communication can suffer without face-to-face contact.
* Blurred boundaries between home and work may lead to burnout.
* Collaboration on complex projects often takes more effort.

**Conclusion:** Remote work is neither a clear win nor a clear loss for productivity. Its success depends on clear expectations, good tools, and a supportive culture. Organizations that invest in these areas are most likely to see lasting gains.

Electric vehicles are quickly moving from the margins to the mainstream. Falling battery costs, expanding charging networks, and stricter emissions rules have all pushed sales higher. For many drivers, the lower running costs and quiet ride are now as attractive as the environmental benefits.

Challenges remain, however. Charging in apartment buildings is still difficult, and long trips require careful planning. Battery production also carries its own environmental footprint, from mining lithium to managing waste at the end of a battery's life.

Even so, the direction of travel is clear. As technology improves and prices fall, electric cars are likely to become the default choice for new buyers. Governments and manufacturers that prepare for this shift now will be best placed to benefit from it.
