Retrieval-augmented generation has emerged as an effective way to ground language model outputs in external knowledge. However, the quality of generated answers depends heavily on the relevance of the retrieved passages, and existing retrievers are rarely optimized for downstream generation. We address this gap by training the retriever with feedback signals derived from the generator's likelihood of producing the correct answer. This yields a retriever that favors passages that are genuinely useful rather than merely similar to the query. On open-domain question answering benchmarks, our method improves exact match by 4.1 points over a fixed retriever and narrows the gap to much larger models. We also show that the learned retriever transfers across generators, which makes the approach practical in realistic deployments.

Aspect-based sentiment analysis requires models to identify opinion targets and determine the polarity expressed toward each. In multi-aspect sentences, however, models often confuse which opinion belongs to which target. We propose a syntax-aware attention mechanism that uses dependency relations to restrict attention to words linked to the target aspect. The mechanism is lightweight and can be added to any encoder. Across four public datasets, it improves macro-F1 by an average of 2.5 points, and the gains are largest on sentences containing several aspects with conflicting polarities. Visualization of attention weights confirms that the model focuses on relevant opinion words. Our results indicate that explicit syntactic structure remains valuable even in the era of pretrained encoders.

Evaluating the factual consistency of generated summaries is a persistent challenge in text summarization. Standard overlap metrics correlate poorly with human judgments of faithfulness and fail to penalize hallucinated content. In this study, we introduce a question-based evaluation protocol that automatically generates questions from the summary and checks whether they can be answered consistently from the source document. We validate the metric against human annotations on three datasets and find that it achieves substantially higher correlation than existing alternatives. Furthermore, we apply the protocol to compare recent summarization systems and show that fluency gains have not been matched by improvements in faithfulness. We make the evaluation toolkit publicly available.

Low-resource neural machine translation suffers from a lack of parallel data, and back-translation is the most widely used remedy. Yet the quality of synthetic data varies widely, and noisy samples can harm training. This paper investigates how to select and weight back-translated sentences so that the most informative examples dominate learning. We design a scoring function that combines model confidence, source-side diversity, and domain similarity to the target task. Experiments on translation between English and four low-resource languages demonstrate gains of up to 2.8 BLEU over uniform back-translation. Detailed analyses show that our selection criteria prefer syntactically diverse sentences, which helps the model generalize to unseen constructions.

Social media text is full of abbreviations, misspellings, and creative orthography that confuse standard natural language processing tools. We study lexical normalization as a preprocessing step and ask whether it still benefits modern contextual models. A character-level sequence model is trained on a new corpus of tweets annotated with canonical word forms. Applied before part-of-speech tagging and parsing, normalization yields modest but consistent improvements, while the benefit for named entity recognition is larger. We conclude that normalization remains useful for noisy domains, although the gap narrows as pretrained models see more informal text during training.

Understanding the emotional content of speech is crucial for building empathetic human-computer interaction systems. In this paper, we present a multimodal approach to emotion recognition that fuses acoustic features with transcribed text using a cross-modal attention network. The acoustic stream captures prosodic cues such as pitch and energy, while the textual stream provides semantic context. We evaluate our model on the IEMOCAP dataset and achieve an unweighted accuracy of 74.3 percent, outperforming previous single-modality and multimodal approaches. An ablation study confirms that both modalities contribute meaningfully, with the fusion mechanism providing the largest additional gain. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of cross-modal attention for emotion recognition.

Coreference resolution is a key component of discourse understanding, but errors in mention detection propagate to downstream clustering. We propose an end-to-end model that jointly learns mention detection and antecedent ranking while incorporating a span-level representation enriched with head-word information. The joint training objective allows the two subtasks to inform each other and reduces error propagation. On the OntoNotes benchmark, our model achieves an average F1 of 73.9, establishing a new state of the art at the time of writing. Error analysis suggests that remaining mistakes primarily involve pronouns with distant antecedents and long, nested mentions. We discuss possible directions for addressing these challenges.

Controllable text generation aims to produce fluent text that satisfies specified attributes such as topic, sentiment, or style. Existing methods typically rely on fine-tuning separate models for each attribute combination, which is expensive and inflexible. We introduce a plug-and-play framework that steers a frozen language model using small attribute classifiers applied at decoding time. Our approach supports the composition of multiple attributes without retraining and offers fine-grained control over the strength of each. Human and automatic evaluations show that generated text exhibits stronger attribute alignment than prior plug-and-play baselines while maintaining comparable fluency and diversity. We further demonstrate applications to detoxification and formality transfer.

Semantic parsing translates natural language utterances into executable logical forms, enabling non-expert users to query databases. Generalization to unseen database schemas, however, remains difficult. In this work, we present a schema-linking module that explicitly aligns question tokens with table and column names using both lexical and structural cues. The module is integrated into a sequence-to-sequence parser with a grammar-constrained decoder. We evaluate on the Spider benchmark and obtain an exact match accuracy of 61.8 percent on the development set, a considerable improvement over comparable systems. Our analysis highlights the importance of accurate schema linking for cross-domain generalization and identifies nested queries as the main source of remaining errors.

Authorship attribution has important applications in forensics, literary studies, and plagiarism detection. We examine whether stylometric features that are effective for long documents remain reliable for short texts. Using a corpus of emails and forum posts from over 500 authors, we compare character n-gram models, function-word frequencies, and neural embeddings across text lengths ranging from 50 to 2,000 words. Results show that accuracy degrades sharply below 200 words, with neural embeddings being the most robust. We also find that topic leakage inflates reported performance in several prior studies. Based on these observations, we recommend evaluation protocols that control for topic and length.
